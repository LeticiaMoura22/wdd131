Container Garden Guide

Site Plan: Project Blueprint

1. Site Name

Container Garden Guide

The name says what the site offers: practical, beginner-friendly help for growing vegetables in pots, on balconies, patios, and windowsills. It is short, easy to remember, and easy to say. Its logo is a small pot with a sprouting plant.

Optional domain availability: containergardenguide.org

2. Site Purpose

The site gives people with little outdoor space a clear starting point for container vegetable gardening. It provides:

  • A short introduction and a get-started checklist.
  • A filterable plant guide listing about 12 vegetables by sun needs, pot size, days to harvest, and planting season.
  • A seasonal planner checklist that saves progress in the browser.
  • A workshop sign-up form and a references page that credits all sources.

3. Scenarios

Visitors are renters or new gardeners with a balcony, patio, or sunny windowsill. They ask:

  1. Which vegetables grow well in a pot on a shady balcony?
  2. What size pot does a tomato plant need, and how many days until harvest?
  3. What should I do this month to keep my container garden on track?
  4. How do I sign up for a beginner workshop?

4. Color Schema

The palette uses soft, natural tones that suit a gardening theme and keep text readable.

  • Leaf Green #2F5D3A

    Headings, header background, and primary buttons. White text on this green has a contrast ratio above 7:1.

  • Terracotta #A8502A

    Accents, links, and the logo pot. Used for text only on the cream background, where it has a contrast ratio above 4.5:1.

  • Potting Cream #FBF7EE

    Page background and cards.

  • Soil Ink #1F2A24

    Body paragraphs. This near-black green has a contrast ratio above 14:1 on the cream background.

5. Typography

Two fonts are used, both from the system font stack so the site loads fast without extra requests.

Headings: Georgia, serif
Choose the right pot for your plants
Body and navigation: Verdana, Geneva, sans-serif
Most vegetables need at least six hours of sun a day. Check the label on each plant before you buy it.
